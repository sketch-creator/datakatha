# House style

Other data desk skills call this one. Apply every rule below unless the user explicitly overrides it.

## Numbers
- Spell out one to nine in prose; use figures for 10 and up, and for all units, percentages and ages.
- Thousands separators: 12,289. Decimals: one place for rates and per capita values (8.7 tonnes), none for totals in the millions or billions.
- Large numbers in prose: 3.2 billion tonnes, 1.4 million people. In charts: 3.2bn, 1.4m in tick labels, full words in the axis title.
- Percent: "48%" in charts and headlines, "48 percent" is also fine in prose if the publication prefers. Percentage points: "5 points" or "5 percentage points", never "5%" for a difference between two percentages.
- Multiples: "4.9 times" or "fivefold" (round only if the exact value is within 0.1). Never say "500% higher" in a headline; say "six times as much".
- Always say what a number is per: per person, per year, per 100,000 residents.
- Round once, at the end, to the nearest value: 1,641.54% is 1,642% and 41.271 is 41.3. Never cut digits off. Keep full precision in calculations.

## Dates and periods
- Months: Jan., Feb., Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov., Dec. with a date; spelled out alone. (Swap for your publication's style if different.)
- Year ranges: "1990 to 2024" in prose, "1990-2024" in axis labels.
- Fiscal years: label them, e.g. "fiscal 2025 (July 2024 to June 2025)".
- Say "latest available year" only together with the actual year.

## Language for data
- Say "linked to" or "associated with", not "caused", unless the source shows cause.
- Say "estimated" when the source calls the figure an estimate.
- Name the source in the first reference to a number: "according to the Global Carbon Budget".
- Avoid "record" or "highest ever" unless the full series has been checked.
- Flag small groups: any comparison built on fewer than 30 observations gets a caveat.

## Headlines and graphic titles
- The title states the finding, with a number when possible: "UK emissions nearly halved since 1990".
- Present tense for current states, past tense for completed changes.
- 60 characters or fewer for chart titles. No questions, no puns.
- The dek, one line, says what is measured, the unit, and the years.

## Charts
Read `chart-rules.md` in this skill for form, color, marks and labels. Colors, fonts and export sizes are in `palette.json`.

## Credit lines
- Source: `Source: <publisher or original source>, <dataset>, <year>` (when data came via an aggregator: `Source: Global Carbon Budget (2025) via Our World in Data`).
- Notes: `Note: <definitions, exclusions, rounding>`.
- Credit: `Graphic: Data Desk` (replace with your desk or byline).
- AI disclosure for any AI-generated illustration: `Illustration generated with AI` under the image. Charts drawn from data by code do not need it.
